# Customers and consent

The people on a shop's list — everyone who has bought something, signed up, or
been added by an automation — are [contacts](/objects/contact), and they are
where the API's write surface is: create-or-update a person, change their tags,
change their list memberships. All three writes are shaped by one principle:
**an automation must not be able to do, at scale, what a seller would not do by
hand.**

## The field that decides everything

<Callout type="error">
**`marketingConsentAt` is whether you may email this person.** A timestamp
means they went through a double opt-in and clicked the link. `null` means
they did not — usually a customer who bought something and was never asked.
`null` is not "unknown"; it is "no".
</Callout>

Nothing an API key sends can set it. A contact created through the API always
has `null` there, whatever the request body says; passing `"sendOptIn": true`
makes Sailo email that person the same double opt-in link the public signup
form uses, and the timestamp is written **when they click** — not when you
asked. The mechanics, including the `optInSent` flag and why it can be `false`
without anything having failed, are on the
[contacts endpoint](/api/contacts#consent).

Syncing into a newsletter tool, filter server-side:

<CurlExample path="/contacts?consented=true&limit=100" />

Everybody the unfiltered list returns who is not in that set is somebody you
may not email. And because consent can arrive *after* the sync that first saw
the person, a nightly re-read of `?consented=true` is part of the job — the
[CRM guide](/guides/sync-a-crm#handle-the-consent-gap) walks through the gap.

## Creating is idempotent by person

`POST /contacts` is create-or-update, matched on email or phone. Sending
somebody twice updates them and merges their tags rather than duplicating or
failing — which is what makes it safe behind a form, a Typeform, or a Zapier
action that may fire twice. There is no separate update endpoint, because there
is nothing for one to do.

Two merge rules that are easy to get backwards:

- **Tags are merged, never replaced.** Sending `["webinar"]` adds it and
  leaves whatever else they carry.
- **A name only ever fills a gap.** If the contact already has a name — from
  the seller, or from an order — yours is ignored. A form field's guess should
  not overwrite what a human knows.

## Tags and lists are different sentences

A tag says what somebody *is*; a list says what they get *sent*. Both
operations are add-and-remove, never replace-the-set — a tag the seller put on
somebody by hand is not something an automation should delete by omitting it.
Both are no-ops when there is nothing to do, so both are safe to retry. The
tag endpoint is [its own route](/api/contacts#tagging-is-its-own-endpoint) for
a practical reason: *tag everyone who turned up* should not require sending a
name and an email you may not have.

Joining a list cannot make somebody a subscriber: on a double opt-in list —
the default — the membership lands `pending` until that person's own click
confirms it. Read the state the response reports rather than assuming the one
you asked for. Counts and the `doubleOptIn` rule are on the
[lists page](/api/lists).

## From a customer's orders to the customer

An order's `customer.clientId` joins to this list, and is `null` on a guest
checkout that matched nobody. `contact.created` fires when somebody new
appears — from an order, a signup or the API alike — and carries the
[same object](/objects/contact) the read endpoints return, `source` saying how
they arrived.

## What is deliberately missing

**Deletion.** There is no `DELETE /contacts/{id}` — a person removed by an
automation is not a mistake the seller can undo, and the one legitimate case
belongs in the admin with a human attached.

**`notes`.** The seller's private scratchpad about a customer is not on the
object in either transport. It is the last thing that should sync into a
third-party CRM through an integration nobody re-read.
