# Working with products

The catalogue is the read-only half of the API: two endpoints, no writes, and —
alone among the resources here — **no webhook**. Products are edited by a person
in the admin, at human speed, and read by integrations. That shape decides
everything on this page.

## The mistake that delists a shop

<Callout type="warning">
**`stock: null` means not counted, not sold out.** A product with
`trackInventory: false` reports `stock: null` because Sailo is not counting
units — a service, a digital download, a made-to-order item. On many shops that
is *most of the catalogue*, and a sync that reads null as zero delists all of
it in one run.
</Callout>

The field to gate a listing on is **`inStock`**, a boolean that already
accounts for `trackInventory`. Use `stock` only for a number you display, and
only where `trackInventory` is true. The three fields and the question each
answers are on the [product object reference](/objects/product).

## Mirroring the catalogue

The pattern, for a marketplace listing, a POS, or anything else that holds a
copy:

1. **Page the list.** `GET /products` returns the catalogue newest first; a
   `limit` of <MaxLimit /> quarters the number of calls a full scan makes.
2. **Fetch detail where variants matter.** The list returns `variants: []` on
   every row, deliberately — `GET /products/{id}` returns them in full. Page
   the list, then fetch detail for the products you actually mirror.
3. **Re-run it on a schedule.** There is no `product.updated` event to wait
   for, so a mirror converges by re-reading. A nightly pass over a
   page or two of catalogue is cheap; a price that is wrong until the next
   manual sync is not.

<CurlExample path="/products?published=true&limit=100" />

## Drafts are readable, and that matters

`isPublished: false` is a draft: invisible on the storefront, readable through
the API. That is correct behaviour for a key the seller minted themselves — and
it means **an unfiltered mirror will publish things the seller has not**.
Filter with `?published=true` unless a draft is specifically what you want,
which a catalogue audit might.

## `kind` decides the rest of the object

One of `physical`, `digital`, `service`, `event`, `membership` — and it decides
which of three optional objects is present: a `service` carries `booking`, an
`event` carries `event`, a `membership` carries `membership`. If your
integration only handles one kind, filter on it:

<CurlExample path="/products?kind=physical&published=true" />

A membership product is what a [subscription](/objects/subscription) is a
subscription *to*, and selling one is where the `subscription.*` events begin —
the [access guide](/guides/membership-access) picks up from there.

## Prices

Products carry no currency of their own — a shop prices in one, and every
[money object](/objects/money) on every product carries the shop's code.
`compareAt` is the struck-through "was" price where the seller set one and
`null` where they did not, not a copy of `price`.

A variant's `price` is **always populated** — one with no price of its own
inherits the product's, so there is no null to handle.

## Store the `id`, display the `slug`

`slug` is the product's URL segment on the storefront, and a seller can change
it. A mirror keyed on it loses the product at the first rename. Store `id`;
treat `slug` as display.

The same rule from the other side: an [order line](/objects/order) keeps its
own `title` and `sku`, and its `productId` is `null` once the seller has
deleted the product. A join back to the catalogue must handle that null rather
than treating it as corrupt data.
