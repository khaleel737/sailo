# Working with orders

An order is the record of a sale — what was bought, by whom, for how much, and
where both the parcel and the money stand. It is the object most integrations
are really about, and the mistakes on this page are the ones that reach
customers.

## Two statuses, two questions

<Callout type="warning">
`status` is **fulfilment** — `new`, `confirmed`, `shipped`, `completed`,
`cancelled`, `refunded`. `paymentStatus` is **money** — `unpaid`, `pending`,
`paid`, `refunded`, `disputed`. **Neither answers the other's question.**
</Callout>

A card order lands `status: "new"` and `paymentStatus: "paid"` in the same
instant, so reading `new` as "not paid" holds back fulfilment on every card
sale. A bank-transfer order can sit `confirmed` and `unpaid` for days, so
reading `confirmed` as "money arrived" ships goods nobody has paid for. The
[orders endpoint](/api/orders) filters on the two separately, and they combine:

<CurlExample path="/orders?status=confirmed&payment_status=paid" />

## React to events, backfill from the list

Five `order.*` events — `order.created`, `order.paid`, `order.shipped`,
`order.cancelled`, `order.refunded` — cover the lifecycle, and every one
carries the **complete order** in `data`: the same body `GET /orders/{id}`
returns, built by the same code. A consumer that receives an event and then
immediately fetches the order is making a call it already had the answer to.

Two things events do not do:

- **The past.** Orders that predate the integration never fired an event you
  received. Page [`GET /orders`](/api/orders) once at setup.
- **Guarantees.** Delivery is at-least-once, [not
  ordered](/webhooks/delivery#ordering), and an endpoint that was down long
  enough has permanently missed deliveries. Upsert whole payloads, deduplicate
  on `webhook-id`, and re-read the list on a schedule if staleness costs you
  money. The [going-live checklist](/guides/going-live) has the full set.

**Fulfil on `order.paid`, not `order.created`.** The created event fires for
unpaid orders too — that is what it is for.

## Money on an order

Six [money objects](/objects/money), and five of them compose:

```
subtotal − discount + deliveryFee + tax = total
```

`refunded` sits outside that sum: it is how much has gone back so far.

<Callout type="info">
**A partial refund leaves `paymentStatus: "paid"`.** Only refunding the whole
order moves it to `refunded`. The reliable reading is arithmetic, not the
status: `total.cents - refunded.cents` is what the seller has kept, and a
non-zero `refunded` on a `paid` order is normal. In `cents` — `amount` is for
display, and [not `cents / 100`](/objects/money).
</Callout>

## The customer on an order is not a contact

`customer` carries what the buyer typed at checkout, and `customer.clientId`
is the join to the shop's list — readable with
[`GET /contacts/{id}`](/api/contacts). It is `null` on a guest checkout that
matched nobody, which is normal rather than an error.

<Callout type="error">
**An order's `customer.email` is not consent to email that person.** It is an
address somebody typed to receive a receipt. Consent lives on the
[contact](/objects/contact#marketingconsentat), and the
[customers guide](/guides/customers) is about exactly this line.
</Callout>

`?email=` finds one person's orders by what they typed at checkout; a customer
who used two addresses is two answers. For everything tied to one person on
the seller's list, match on `customer.clientId` instead.

## Lines survive the catalogue

`items` is populated on both endpoints and on every webhook — there is no
`?expand=`. Each line keeps its own `title`, `sku` and prices, and its
`productId` is `null` once the seller has deleted the product. An order is a
record of what was sold, not a join to a catalogue that has moved on; handle
the null. `itemCount` is total **units**, not lines.

## Shipping

`order.shipped` fires when the seller marks the order shipped, and
`delivery.shippedAt` is set at the same moment — so a consumer that missed the
event can still tell by reading. `trackingCarrier` is free text, because
sellers ship with local couriers Sailo has never heard of: show it, do not
switch on it.
