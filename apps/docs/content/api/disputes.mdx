# Disputes

A chargeback: a buyer telling their bank to reverse a sale.

The reason to read these over an API is the clock. A dispute allows about twenty
days to respond, and the evidence that answers it almost always lives somewhere
that is not Sailo — a helpdesk thread, a fulfilment tool, a shipping account.
These endpoints are what let a seller's own tooling go and fetch it before the
deadline rather than after.

## A chargeback is not a refund

<Callout type="error">
A refund is the seller choosing to give money back. A chargeback is a bank
taking it, plus a fee, and usually without asking. They land on the same order
and they mean opposite things about the customer relationship — so never
describe one as the other in an alert, a dashboard or a message to a seller.
</Callout>

On the order, a chargeback shows as `paymentStatus: "disputed"`. That is not
`refunded` and must not be rendered as it.

## `caseType` decides whether anything has been lost yet

**`inquiry`** — a retrieval request. The issuer is asking a question on the
cardholder's behalf. **No money has moved**, no fee has been charged, and
answering it well often ends the matter. An alert that says "you have lost a
sale" here is wrong and will send a seller into a panic over nothing.

**`chargeback`** — the money is already gone. `deducted` is what actually left
the balance: the sale plus Stripe's dispute fee, which is why a £42 chargeback
costs £57.

Both arrive through the same `dispute.opened` event, so branch on `caseType`
before you tell anybody anything.

## The three amounts

| Field | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `amount` | The disputed sale. |
| `fee` | Stripe's dispute fee. Charged on a chargeback, not on an inquiry. |
| `deducted` | Amount plus fee — what left the seller's balance. |

`fundsWithdrawnAt` says when. On a win, `fundsReinstatedAt` says when they came
back.

## `dueBy` is the field to build on

It is the response deadline, and it is null only on a case that no longer needs
one. The useful automation is: on `dispute.opened`, read `dueBy`, and open a
task in whatever the seller actually works out of with that date on it.

## Only the seller's own sales

This endpoint returns disputes where a buyer charged back one of the shop's
sales. It never returns a *platform* dispute — a seller charging back their own
Sailo subscription — because that is our money and our argument with them, and
it has no business arriving in their Zapier account dressed as a customer
chargeback. There is no filter that changes this.

## What is deliberately absent

**The evidence bundle.** `evidenceSnapshot` is the complete case assembled to
answer the dispute: buyer address, delivery proof, the seller's own account of
events. It exists to be sent to Stripe, not syndicated to whatever an
integration points at. `completenessBp` tells you how strong the response was —
in basis points, over the fields the case required — without shipping the
response itself.

**Responding.** You cannot submit evidence through this API. Answering a dispute
is a one-shot action with a deadline and a fee attached, and it belongs in the
admin where both are in front of the person doing it.

**Stripe identifiers**, for the reason they are absent everywhere else: they
address the seller's account.
