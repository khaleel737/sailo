# Subscriptions

A membership: one buyer paying a shop on a cycle, for a product, until they stop.

These endpoints exist because the seven `subscription.*` webhooks did not have
them. An event that carries an id into a system with no way to resolve it is
half an integration — you could be told a membership changed, and had nowhere to
ask what it changed *to*.

## Revoke on `currentPeriodEnd`, never on the cancellation

<Callout type="error">
`subscription.cancelled` and `cancelAtPeriodEnd: true` both mean **the member
asked to stop**. They have paid through `currentPeriodEnd` and keep their access
until it. Revoking when you see the cancellation takes away a month somebody
already bought.
</Callout>

The sequence for a paid community is:

1. `cancelAtPeriodEnd` flips to `true`, and `canceledAt` is stamped. Change
   nothing — they are still a member.
2. `currentPeriodEnd` passes and the membership ends. `subscription.ended`
   fires. **Now** remove the Discord role.

`status` alone will not tell you this. A cancelled-but-still-running membership
and an ended one can both read `canceled`, and the date is the only thing that
separates them.

## `past_due` is not "gone"

A card that failed this morning puts the membership in `past_due`. Stripe will
retry it, and most of them succeed. An integration that revokes access on the
first failed charge takes it away from somebody whose second attempt clears that
afternoon — and Sailo will not tell you to give it back, because from its point
of view nothing ended.

`trialing`, `active` and `past_due` are the three statuses in which somebody
still has what they paid for.

## `billingMode` changes what renewal means

**`stripe`** — a card, on the seller's connected account. It renews itself, and
`subscription.renewed` follows.

**`manual`** — Sailo raises a renewal order and a human settles it, at the door
or by bank transfer. Nothing will ever arrive from Stripe about one of these, so
an integration waiting for a card renewal on a manual membership waits for ever.
`paymentMethod` names the rail they actually pay on.

The two are otherwise identical. Access, grace and cancellation all read
`status` and `currentPeriodEnd` and never ask how those came to be written,
which is exactly what a consumer should do too.

## Prices are snapshotted

`price` and `intervalCount` are what *this member* agreed to, not what the
product costs now. A seller who re-prices a membership has not re-priced the
people already on it. If you are reconciling revenue, this is the number that
was actually charged.

## What is deliberately absent

**Every Stripe identifier.** `stripeSubscriptionId`, `stripeCustomerId` and
`stripeAccountId` all name objects in the *seller's* Stripe account, and
shipping them would let anything holding a payload address that account
directly. The membership's own `id` is the handle, and it is the one the
webhooks speak too.

**Writes.** There is no way to cancel, pause or re-price a membership through
this API. Those change what somebody is charged, and they belong in the admin
where the seller can see the consequence before they commit to it.
