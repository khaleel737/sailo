## What the surface is, and is not

**Mostly reads.** Three of the <EndpointCount /> operations write, and all three
are on contacts: create-or-update a person, add-or-remove tags, and join-or-leave
lists. That is the whole write surface and it is a deliberate line rather than a
roadmap gap — those three are what an automation actually performs, and
everything else an integration needs is a read.

**No `PUT`, `PATCH` or `DELETE`.** Creating a contact that already exists
updates it, so there is no separate update verb to get wrong; and nothing here
deletes anything, because a deletion arriving from an automation nobody re-read
is not a mistake a seller can undo.

**Every object a webhook carries can now be fetched.** That was not true until
subscriptions and disputes got endpoints: nine of the <EventCount /> events
delivered an object with nowhere to go and read it back, so a consumer that
missed a delivery had lost that state for good. An event and an endpoint are two
halves of one integration, and shipping only the first is what
[spec 16](/webhooks) calls half of one.

**Ten resources, not everything.** Shops, orders, products, contacts, lists,
subscriptions, disputes, bookings, staff and flows. Broadcasts, coupons, tax
registrations, tickets and testimonials have no endpoints — if you need one, the
[support page](/support) says where to ask.
