Forty-one comments saying "mine po" and you've been paid for three of them. Two people are arguing about who was first. One is asking if ₱280 is your last price, on an item you already marked sold.

Buy-and-sell groups are still the biggest, cheapest, most reliable source of buyers a small Filipino seller has, and they run on rules that no platform ever wrote down.

Selling in Facebook groups works when you do three things: post in the group's own listing format with the price visible, treat a "mine" comment as an inquiry rather than a sale, and move anything with numbers in it out of the comment thread and onto a page. That last one is the difference between a busy post and a paid one. A Sailo shop is free at $0 for up to {{free_products}} products, and the reason it matters here is that a link plus a price survives an admin deleting your post.

## Read the pins before you post anything

Every buy-and-sell group is somebody's house. The admin makes the rules, the rules are in the pinned post or the About section, and the rules are enforced by deleting you without explanation.

The ones that actually vary, and that will get your post pulled:

- **Links.** Many groups ban external links outright. Some allow them in comments but not in the post. A few don't care. This is the single most common reason a good listing vanishes.
- **Post format.** Price and location are usually mandatory. "PM for price" is banned in most serious groups and rightly so.
- **Frequency.** One post per member per day is a common cap. Some are one per week.
- **Categories.** Plenty of groups are strictly preloved, or strictly within one city, or ban resellers entirely.

Spend ten minutes reading the pinned rules of the five groups you post in most. It's the highest-value ten minutes in this entire article, because everything else you do is worthless if the post gets removed at 6am.

## Use the listing format, not a wall post

Facebook gives buy-and-sell groups a structured selling post: a photo, a title, a price, a location, a description, and the ability to mark it Available, Pending or Sold. Some groups have it switched on, some don't, and the details differ by region and change over time. If your group has it, use it.

It does two things a normal post can't. It forces the price and the location into fields, which is exactly what the admin was going to nag you about anyway. And marking something Sold updates the post itself, so people stop commenting on an item that's gone.

If the group has it turned off, write your post as though it were on:

```
Preloved Uniqlo kids' tee, size 110
₱180
Cebu City, meet up Ayala or J&T ship nationwide
Good condition, no stains, worn twice
Sold to first to pay
```

Five lines. Price in line two. Location in line three. Nobody has to ask you anything before deciding.

## "Mine po" is an inquiry, not a sale

This is the part that eats sellers alive, and it deserves saying flatly.

The first person to comment "mine" is not your buyer. The first person to send money is your buyer. Everyone in the Philippines who has sold in groups for more than a month knows what a joy reserver is: the person who claims three items enthusiastically, gets your account details, and is never heard from again. It isn't malice. It's a comment that costs nothing to type.

So build the policy into the post, in one line, before anyone comments:

> Sold to first to pay. Reservation needs a ₱50 down payment, deducted from the total.

That line does more work than any customer service you'll do afterwards. A ₱50 down payment on a ₱280 item filters out roughly everyone who was never going to pay, and the ones who do pay it are almost never the ones who ghost you.

The order of operations that keeps you sane: comment, then a price and link reply, then payment, then reserve. Not comment, then reserve, then chase. If you reserve on the strength of a comment you will spend your evenings sending "hi po, still interested?" to people who have moved on.

## What to do when the group bans links

Half the good groups will delete a post with a URL in it. That's their right and it's usually because they've been burned by scam links.

Three things that work, in order of how well:

1. **Put the shop name in the post as text, not as a link.** `sailo.store/rheapreloved` typed out is not a link, doesn't trip most filters, and a buyer can still type it. Make it one word so they can.
2. **Put the link in the first comment on your own post.** Allowed in a lot of groups where post links aren't. Check the rules first.
3. **Send the link when they message you.** Slower, but it's one reply instead of a conversation.

The reason to bother at all: a group post lasts a day, gets buried, and can be deleted by an admin who didn't like your third post that week. A page with your prices on it doesn't. Every group post is either an advert for something permanent, or a one-off transaction after which you start from zero tomorrow.

## Which groups are actually worth your time

Group size is close to meaningless. A 400,000-member national group where posts are buried in ninety seconds is worth less than an 8,000-member group for your own city where the admin removes scammers.

What to check before you commit to posting in one:

| Signal | Good | Bad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Posts per hour | Slow enough that yours is visible for hours | Your post is gone in two minutes |
| Admin activity | Rules enforced, scammers removed, posts approved fast | Nobody has posted as admin in months |
| Comment quality | People asking sizes and arranging meet-ups | Nothing but "F" and tagged friends |
| Geography | Your city or your delivery zone | National, when you only meet up |
| Approval queue | Under an hour | Two days, which kills anything time-sensitive |

Four good groups beats twenty. You'll get more from posting three times a week in a Cebu-specific preloved group than from spraying the same listing into forty national ones, and you'll get banned less.

## The bump problem

Group posts sink. Everyone knows it, so everyone bumps, and most groups now ban bumping because it turned every thread into "up", "up", "up".

What works instead, without breaking rules:

- **Reply to real comments with real information.** A genuine answer bumps the post naturally and is useful to the next reader.
- **Post at the time your buyers are on their phones.** For most Philippine buy-and-sell groups that's evening, after dinner, and Sunday afternoon. Test your own two windows for a fortnight.
- **Repost with a different photo, not the same one.** A new angle is a new post. The same photo twice reads as spam to both the admin and the members.
- **Update the listing to Sold when it's sold.** Sounds like housekeeping. It's actually reputation, because the group sees you complete things.

## Rhea in Cebu: preloved kids' clothes, ₱220 average

Rhea sells preloved branded kids' clothing in Cebu. She buys bales, sorts, photographs, and sells pieces from ₱150 to ₱450, averaging around ₱220. She posts in five groups, three of them Cebu-only.

Her old routine was a photo dump of thirty items in one album post, priced in the caption as a block, with buyers commenting item numbers. It generated enormous comment counts and constant confusion. Two people claiming item 14. Somebody paying for item 9 when they meant item 19. Her Sunday nights were spent scrolling comment threads with a notebook.

She changed three things.

First, she stopped putting thirty items in one post. Now she posts four to six pieces, each with its own photo and price, in the group's listing format where it exists.

Second, she put the full week's stock on a page at `sailo.store/rheapreloved`, one product per piece with the size and condition on it, and her group posts became: photo, price, "full set on rheapreloved dot sailo dot store", meet-up and shipping details. In the two groups that ban links she types the address rather than linking it.

Third, the ₱50 down payment line. This was the one that changed her week.

The numbers she gave: before, about 45 "mine" comments a week turning into roughly 18 paid orders. After, fewer comments, around 30, and 26 paid orders. Same stock, same photos, less noise, and about four hours a week back.

Buyers pay her by GCash, which they were always going to do and which Sailo has nothing to do with. She puts her GCash number in the bank transfer instructions field so it shows at checkout, and every Sunday she sits down with the GCash history on one side and her order list on the other and ticks them off by hand.

## Meet-ups, COD and the money

The money side is where Philippine group selling is genuinely different from the US and UK articles you'll find on this, and where most of them are useless.

**Meet-up.** Cash, in person, at a mall or a station. No fees, no risk of a fake screenshot, and it costs you an hour and a jeepney fare. Set two fixed meet-up windows a week rather than agreeing to each one individually, or you will spend your life at Ayala.

**GCash before shipping.** Fast and normal, and the reason the down payment rule works. Screenshots can be edited, so check your own GCash app, not the image they sent you. Ten seconds of looking at your own balance is worth more than any amount of staring at a forwarded receipt.

**COD through a courier.** The default expectation for provincial buyers and the reason a lot of orders happen at all. It also means you've paid for packing and shipping before you know whether anyone will accept the parcel. Failed deliveries are a cost of doing business here, not an anomaly. What to do about the rate, and how to keep it from eating your margin, is in [cash on delivery for small sellers](/en/blog/cash-on-delivery-for-small-sellers).

One specific thing worth doing: for COD orders from a group, message the buyer the day before delivery. Not to be nice. A buyer who confirms they'll be home is dramatically less likely to be the one who refuses the parcel, and the message costs you thirty seconds against a shipping fee you eat twice if it comes back.

## Being believed is most of the job

In a group full of strangers, the seller who looks safe wins the order at the same price. Almost none of that comes from your product photos.

What moves it: your profile being a real one with history, a highlight of past transactions, replies from buyers you've dealt with, and posting from the same account consistently instead of a new one every few months. Group members remember names. Being the person who marks things Sold and shows up at the meet-up is a slow asset and it compounds.

## What to change before you post again

1. Open the five groups you post in and read the pinned rules. Note which ones allow links.
2. Rewrite your standard post into five lines with the price on line two.
3. Add one line: sold to first to pay, reservation needs a down payment.
4. Put your stock on one page with prices, and make the address one word you can say out loud.
5. Set two fixed meet-up windows and put them in the post.

Then count, for two weeks, how many "mine" comments turn into money. That ratio is the only group metric worth tracking, and the down payment line moves it more than anything else you can do.
