Your video did 92,000 views overnight. Eleven people commented "where do I buy this", four of them twice, and this morning you have zero orders and a notification tab you can't clear.

That gap has one cause. TikTok is excellent at making people want something and gives you almost nowhere to sell it.

You sell on TikTok without TikTok Shop by accepting that split and building for it: TikTok does discovery, and a page you control does the price, the options and the order. The bridge is your bio link, a spoken shop name, and a caption that assumes half your viewers will never tap anything. That's the whole method. A Sailo shop is free at $0 for up to 20 products and lives at `sailo.store/yourname`, which matters here for one reason you'll see in a minute: it's short enough to say out loud in a video.

## TikTok Shop isn't available everywhere, and that's often the whole answer

Before anything else, check whether you can even register. TikTok sells to viewers in far more countries than it lets sellers open a shop in. As of mid-2026 it was live in roughly nineteen markets, the US, the UK, most of Southeast Asia and a growing list in Europe, while sellers in the Gulf and much of Africa still had no local Seller Center to sign up to.

The list changes every few months, so don't take my word or a blog's. Open TikTok Seller Center, try to register from your own country, and see what happens. Two minutes of that beats an afternoon of reading.

If it won't let you in, you're not doing a workaround. You're doing what sellers in most of the world do by default.

## What TikTok actually gives you: three places to put a link, and only one works

This is the part people get wrong, and it wastes weeks.

**Captions are not clickable.** You can type your URL in the caption. It renders as text. Nobody can tap it, and a viewer holding a phone on a bus is not going to retype it.

**Comments are not clickable either.** Same deal. You can pin your own comment to the top of the thread, which is genuinely useful real estate, but the link in it is dead text.

**The bio link is clickable.** That's it. That's your one tappable path off the platform, and it sits two taps away: profile, then link.

Whether the website field even appears in your Edit profile has changed more than once. It has been gated behind a follower count for personal accounts, and Business accounts have been the reliable route to getting it. Go and look at your own profile rather than trusting any guide, including this one. If the field isn't there, switching your account type is the first thing to try, and it costs you nothing except access to some of the music library.

## Make your handle and your shop link the same word

Most sellers only work this out after about thirty videos: the people who buy from a TikTok mostly never tap the link at all.

They watch, they want it, they get distracted, and two days later they remember the product and not where they saw it. If the only route back to you is a link they didn't tap, that sale is gone. If your handle is `@velvetlashco` and your shop is `sailo.store/velvetlashco`, they can find you from memory, from a screenshot, from a friend saying "that lash girl".

> One word, said out loud, spelled on screen, and identical everywhere. That's worth more than any link placement, because a link only works while the viewer is still watching.

So: same string in your TikTok handle, your bio link, your Instagram, and the caption you paste at the bottom of every video. Boring. Effective.

## Write the caption for the people who will never tap

A caption is doing two jobs at once and most sellers only write for one of them.

Job one is search. TikTok's search box gets used the way Google's does, especially for products, and your caption plus your on-screen text is most of what TikTok has to match against. "New drop" tells it nothing. "Cluster lashes for beginners, DIY at home, £14" tells it a lot.

Job two is the memory. Put the price in the caption. Put the price on screen. Say the price out loud. A viewer who knows the number decides in the video, and a viewer who has to go and find out decides never.

The three things that belong in every selling caption:

1. What it is, in the words a buyer would type into search.
2. The price, as a number.
3. Where to get it, as a word they can remember.

That's it. Hashtag stacks of thirty tags are theatre.

## The pinned comment is the second-best space you have

You can pin one comment on your own video. Use it, and don't use it for a link.

Use it to answer the question that will otherwise arrive forty times. If the video is about a candle, the pinned comment says the burn time and the price. If it's about a dress, it says the sizes in stock. Every question you pre-answer is a DM you don't have to type at midnight.

The second use is stock. When something sells out, edit the pin: "the sage one is gone, restocking Friday." Costs six seconds and stops the flood of people asking for something you can't send.

## What to do when a video takes off at 2am

Nobody covers this part, and it's the part that actually decides whether the views turn into money.

A video that's climbing behaves differently from a video that has landed. The first hour of a spike is when your bio link gets the most traffic it will ever get, and it's also, reliably, the hour when you're asleep.

What holds up while you're not there:

- **Stock counts on the page.** If you sold twelve of the eight you have, you're refunding people from a position of weakness. Set the number.
- **A delivery option that covers the whole country, not just your city.** Spikes go national. Local pickup is not an answer for someone in another region.
- **A price that survives volume.** If you priced for six orders a week and get sixty, your postage assumption breaks first.
- **One line in the shop that says when you ship.** "Orders ship Tuesday and Friday" costs you nothing and prevents forty "has it shipped" messages.

The morning after is where the real work is: the going-viral aftermath, including what to do about the orders you can't fulfil, is its own problem and we've written it up in [what to do when a post goes viral](/en/blog/what-to-do-when-a-post-goes-viral).

## Sell the same five things in every video

TikTok pushes new viewers at you constantly. That means most people watching any given video have never seen you before, which has a consequence sellers resist: repetition isn't boring to your audience, because your audience is new every time.

Pick your five best products and make them the only things you sell on camera for a month. Same five. Different videos, different angles, different hooks, same five items. You'll feel like a broken record. Your viewers will be seeing it for the first time, and your bio link will finally match what people arrive expecting.

The seller who films twenty products once each has twenty videos and no recognisable shop. The seller who films five products four times each has the same twenty videos and a brand.

## Deja in Atlanta: $28 lash kits, about 60 orders a month

Deja sells DIY cluster lash kits from Atlanta. One core product at $28, two add-ons at $9 and $14. She posts four times a week, mostly application videos shot on her bathroom counter, and about one in fifteen does more than 50,000 views.

Her first three months were the standard failure. Views were fine. Orders came through DMs, one at a time, and each one took six messages: price, shade, address, total, "I'll send my Cash App", then a screenshot she had to check. She was doing maybe fifteen orders a month off numbers that should have done far more, and she assumed the problem was the product.

The problem was the path. There were four taps and a conversation between "I want that" and "I paid", and each one lost people.

What changed, in order:

1. She switched to a Business account so the website field appeared, and put `sailo.store/dejalashes` in it.
2. She changed her handle to match. Same word, everywhere.
3. She started saying "deja lashes dot sailo dot store" at the end of every video and putting it on screen for the last three seconds.
4. She put the $28, the two add-ons, and a flat $5 US shipping on the page, with a note that orders ship Monday and Thursday.

Orders went to about 60 a month across the same posting schedule. The interesting part is where they came from: her link taps went up, but the bigger jump was people arriving at the shop directly, having typed the name in, sometimes days after the video. That traffic didn't exist before, because before there was nothing to type.

She still gets DMs. She answers them with a link and four words.

## When TikTok Shop is genuinely the better answer

This article would be dishonest if it ended with "so never use TikTok Shop". Sometimes it wins, clearly.

An in-video cart is a shorter path than any link you will ever write. The buyer taps the product tag, pays inside the app with a card TikTok already has, and never leaves the feed. Nothing you build off-platform beats that for a cheap impulse item.

| Your situation | Probably better |
| --- | --- |
| $9 phone cases, high volume, TikTok Shop live in your country | TikTok Shop |
| Made-to-order, custom sizing, long questions before purchase | Your own page |
| You need the buyer's details for repeat business | Your own page |
| You want reach from Shop's own product surfaces and affiliates | TikTok Shop |
| No Seller Center in your country | Your own page, and it isn't close |

The costs of Shop are real too, and worth checking rather than guessing: it takes a referral fee on every sale, the rate has been changed several times since launch, registration wants business documents, and your fulfilment performance is scored by rules you don't set. Open Seller Center and read the current fee schedule yourself. Anyone quoting you a percentage from memory, including a blog post from last year, is quoting a number that has probably moved.

Plenty of sellers run both. Shop for the cheap volume item, own page for everything with options and questions.

## Do this to your next video

Before you post the next one, in this order:

1. Check whether your Edit profile screen has a website field. If not, switch account type.
2. Make your handle and your shop link the same word, then change nothing about it again.
3. Put the price on screen, in the caption, and in your voice.
4. Say the shop name in the last three seconds. Not "link in bio". The actual name.
5. Pin a comment with the one thing people always ask.

Do that for two weeks and watch where your shop traffic comes from. If direct visits start showing up alongside link taps, the name is doing its job, and you've stopped depending on a tap you can't control.
