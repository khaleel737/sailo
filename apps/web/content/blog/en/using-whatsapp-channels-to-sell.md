You've got 340 people in a WhatsApp group and every time you post this week's rate, four of them reply "ok", one sends a good morning image, and somebody's uncle starts a conversation about the cricket.

A channel fixes that specific problem and creates a different one.

A WhatsApp channel is a one-way broadcast. Only you post. Followers can't reply, can't message you, and can't see each other. You can't see them either, which is the part sellers discover three weeks in. Used properly it's an excellent price board: this week's almonds are ₹640 a kilo, here's the link, orders close Friday. Used as a shop, it fails, because a channel has no way to take an order. The order has to land somewhere else, which is where a page with your prices on it does the work.

## A channel is not a group, a broadcast list, or your catalogue

These four things get mixed up constantly and they behave completely differently. This table is worth more than the rest of the section.

| | Who can post | Who they see | Reaches | Replies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Channel** | Only admins | Nobody sees anybody | Anyone who follows the link, unlimited | None. Emoji reactions and polls only |
| **Group** | Everyone | Members see each other's numbers | People you add or who join a link | Everyone, constantly |
| **Broadcast list** | You | Recipients see only you | Only contacts who have your number saved, up to 256 | Yes, as a normal private chat |
| **Business catalogue** | You | Anyone in a chat with you | Whoever opens your profile | It's a list, not a message |

Three consequences fall straight out of that table.

A **broadcast list** only delivers to people who have saved your number. If a customer messaged you once and never added you to contacts, your broadcast never arrives, and you'll never know. That's the single most misunderstood thing in WhatsApp selling, and it's why sellers with "1,200 contacts" get eleven replies.

A **group** gives you replies and gives everyone else your customers' phone numbers. In a buy-and-sell context that's a feature until it isn't.

A **channel** gives you clean, quiet reach and takes away every form of contact in exchange.

## What a channel actually gives you

Followers see your updates in the Updates tab, separate from their chats. You can post text, photos, videos, voice notes, links, stickers and polls. Followers can react with an emoji and vote in a poll, and that's the entire extent of what they can do back.

Nobody's phone number is exposed in either direction. Followers stay anonymous to you, and you stay unmessageable to them.

A channel isn't an archive either, so don't build anything around old posts staying findable. Treat every update as disposable, because it is.

The reach is quieter than a group message. A channel update goes to the Updates tab rather than lighting up someone's chat list, and most people won't have notifications on for it. Plan for a noticeboard people check, not an alert you can fire at will. That's a downgrade in urgency and an upgrade in tolerance: nobody has ever left a channel because it posted twice this week, and plenty of people have left a group for exactly that.

## Every post has to carry the way back to you

Since a follower cannot reply, the only route from your update to an order is a link you put in the update. There is no other route. If you post a photo of this week's stock with no link, an interested follower has literally nothing to tap.

Two links do all the work:

- **Your shop page**, for anything with a price and options. The buyer picks, fills in the address, and the order arrives written out.
- **A wa.me link to your own number**, for anything that needs a conversation. It opens a normal chat with you, which the channel itself can never do.

Put one of the two in every single post. Not most posts. Every one.

> A channel with no link in the post is a poster with the phone number torn off.

If the order is going to land in your WhatsApp anyway, it's worth making sure it lands already written rather than as "hi, price?". That mechanic is covered properly in [how to take orders on WhatsApp](/en/blog/how-to-take-orders-on-whatsapp).

## What to post, and how often

Two posts a week, at the same time, beats seven scattered ones. People learn a rhythm faster than they learn a brand.

What earns a post:

1. **A price change or this week's rate.** The single best use of a channel. It's information, not advertising, and people follow for it.
2. **A restock.** "Kesar mangoes back Thursday, 2kg boxes, ₹880" with a link. Short.
3. **A closing time.** "Orders for Sunday delivery close 6pm Friday." Deadlines do more than discounts.
4. **A poll.** "Next batch: cashew or pista?" Two taps, and you get a stock decision made for you by the people who'll buy it.
5. **One post in four that isn't selling.** The godown at 6am, a bad batch you rejected, the packing line before a festival. This is what stops the channel becoming a coupon feed.

What doesn't earn a post: good morning messages, motivational quotes, forwarded festival graphics, and "hi everyone, hope you're well". Every one of those trains people to swipe past you, and swiping past is a habit that carries over to the post where you actually had something to sell.

## Growing a channel when nobody can find you

Channel discovery inside WhatsApp exists and it mostly serves large accounts. You are not going to be found by browsing. Every follower you get, you'll bring.

Where they come from, roughly in order of what works:

- **Your Instagram bio and stories.** The channel link is a URL like any other. A story frame saying "weekly rates on WhatsApp, no spam, link here" converts better than you'd expect, because people trust WhatsApp more than they trust an email list.
- **A card in the parcel.** Printed, with the link as a QR code. Your best customers are the ones already holding your product.
- **Your WhatsApp Business profile and away message.** Anyone who messages you sees it.
- **Forwards.** A follower can forward one of your updates into a chat or group, and the forward carries a way back to the channel. That's your only organic growth mechanic, so make at least one post a month worth forwarding: a genuinely good rate, a useful piece of information, something a person would send to their sister.

Don't buy followers, don't add people, and don't announce the channel more than twice a month on your other accounts. A channel with 200 people who want the rates is worth ten times one with 3,000 who forgot they followed.

## Imran in Hyderabad: weekly rates, ₹640 a kilo

Imran sells dry fruits and dates in Hyderabad. Almonds, cashews, pista, khajoor, plus gift boxes around Diwali and Ramzan. Rates move week to week, which is exactly why a channel suits him.

He used to run a WhatsApp group of about 400 customers. It worked, and it was awful. Every rate post drew twenty replies, half of them "ok ji", and two customers had once got into an argument in it that he had to mediate at 11pm. Customers could see each other's numbers, and a competitor joined the group and quietly messaged his list.

He moved to a channel in about ten minutes and told the group once, with the link, before archiving it.

His posting rhythm now: Tuesday 7pm, this week's rates as one image with the numbers on it, plus the shop link. Friday 6pm, one line, "orders for Sunday close at 9". Once a month, a poll or a photo from the market. That's it.

The rates post looks like this:

```
This week
Almonds (Mamra) 640/kg
Cashew W240 1,150/kg
Pista roasted 1,480/kg
Ajwa khajoor 950/500g
Order: imrandryfruits.sailo.store
Sunday delivery, Hyderabad city, orders close Friday 9pm
```

Orders arrive on the page, not in his chat. He gets paid by UPI, which every customer already uses and which has nothing to do with Sailo, and he matches the UTR to the order himself on Saturday mornings.

Two numbers he tracks. The channel sits at about 520 followers, up from a 400-person group. Sunday deliveries went from around 30 to around 45 a week, and he attributes almost none of that to the channel being magic. His words: the rates post was always the thing people wanted, and the group was drowning it in noise.

The thing that surprised him: he can't tell who his followers are. Not one name. During Ramzan he wanted to send an offer to his best customers, and there was no way to do it from the channel at all. He had to use his order history, which lives in the shop, not in WhatsApp.

## What you give up, and why it matters more than it sounds

This is the honest part, and it's structural rather than a niggle.

**You cannot contact your followers.** Ever. There is no list, no numbers, no way to message one person. A channel audience feels like yours and is not portable in any sense. If WhatsApp changed the rules tomorrow, or your channel went sideways, those 520 people would be unreachable.

**You cannot see who is engaged.** Emoji counts and poll votes, that's your entire analytics. You will not know that your best customer read every post.

**You cannot have a conversation.** A follower with a question has to find your wa.me link and start a chat, which is a step, and steps lose people.

The way to live with all three: treat the channel as reach, and treat your order history as the actual asset. Every person who orders gives you a name, an address and a phone number, and that list is yours in a way the follower count never will be. ## Where Sailo stops

There's no UPI rail. None. Sailo's payment rails are card, bank transfer, cash on delivery, and chat rails like WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram, email and phone. UPI is not one of them, and in India that's the rail that actually matters.

What Imran does, and what you'd do, is put the UPI ID into the bank transfer instructions field so buyers see it at checkout with the amount. Then you reconcile by hand. Sailo can record that the order exists and that you marked it paid. It cannot see your bank, so it cannot tell you the money landed. Only your bank app can do that.

## Set the channel up this week

1. Create the channel from the Updates tab. Name it what customers call you, not a slogan.
2. Write a description with one line about what you post and how often. "Weekly rates, Tuesdays. Orders close Friday."
3. Put your shop link in the description and in every post.
4. Post the rates once. Then don't post again for three days.
5. Share the link in your Instagram bio, your WhatsApp Business profile, and on a card in the next twenty parcels.

Give it a month at two posts a week before you judge it.
