You post a photo. Someone likes it enough to tap your profile. They find the link. They tap it, and land on a page of buttons — WhatsApp, another shop, a form, a newsletter. They tap again, and now they are somewhere else entirely, loading a second site on a train with two bars of signal.

Somewhere in there, most of them leave.

## The tap you cannot afford

Every step between "I want that" and "I paid for that" leaks buyers. The leak is worst at the point where the buyer has to leave the app they were happily in. That is the moment they remember they were doing something else.

A link tree adds a step by design. It exists to point somewhere; it never *is* the destination. So the question is not whether your link tree is pretty — it is whether the thing on the other side of it is a shop.

## What a buyer should see

The page a buyer lands on should answer three questions before they scroll:

1. What do you sell?
2. What does it cost?
3. How do I get it?

If any of those needs another tap, it is a question the buyer answers by leaving.

> A price on the page is not a detail. It is the difference between browsing and buying.

## Fewer pages, more orders

The fix is not a better link tree. It is not having one. Put the products, the prices and the order button on the page the link already goes to, and the journey is: post, tap, buy.

Three steps. No handoff, no second site, no loading spinner on a train.

That is the whole idea behind Sailo — one link that *is* the shop, rather than one that points at it.
