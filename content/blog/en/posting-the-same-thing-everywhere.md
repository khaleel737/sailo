You made one video on Sunday. It went to TikTok, then Reels, then a Facebook page, then a Short, and by Wednesday three of the four had done nothing and you could not work out which part was the problem.

Cross-posting is not the mistake. Cross-posting the *file* is.

Repeat the idea, the product, the price and the shop link everywhere, every time, without apology. Re-export the video for each platform instead of downloading it from one and uploading it to the next, rewrite the first line of every caption, and delete any phrase that only makes sense somewhere else. That is most of the work, it takes about twenty minutes a week, and it is the difference between four posts and one post shown four times badly. A Sailo shop is $0 on the free plan for up to 20 products and sits at `sailo.store/yourname`, which is the one thing that genuinely should be identical on all four.

## What is safe to repeat and what is not

Two lists. The first one is where sellers waste time worrying, and the second is where they actually lose reach.

**Repeat these, deliberately, forever:**

- The product and the price.
- The shop name, spelled the same way, in your voice and on screen.
- Your best-performing idea. Your audience is new every week; you are the only person bored of it.
- The core of the video: the same hands, the same product, the same fifteen seconds.

**Do not repeat these:**

- The exported file, saved from one app and uploaded to another.
- The caption, word for word.
- The aspect ratio.
- The call to action, if it names a feature the next platform does not have.
- The timing, if one of your platforms is evergreen and the others are not.

## The watermark is the one everybody can see

Save a video out of TikTok and it comes with a watermark and a username stamped on it, moving around the frame. Upload that to Reels and two things happen. Meta has said for years that it shows recycled, visibly watermarked video less, and separately, every human who sees it knows exactly what you did.

The second one costs more than the first. A watermark from another app is a small, clear signal that this post was not made for the person watching it. On a platform where you are asking a stranger to trust you with money, that is a bad first impression to hand out for free.

The fix is dull. Export from your editor once per platform, at the right size, with no watermark, and upload each file separately. If you edit in the app itself, take the extra two minutes to save the clean version before you add the platform's own text and stickers. Ten seconds of habit at the start of the process removes a problem you cannot remove later.

## Aspect ratio, and the crop that eats your price

Four common shapes, and they are not interchangeable:

| Where | Shape | What gets cut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok, Reels, Shorts, Stories | 9:16 vertical | Nothing, if you built it there |
| Instagram feed post | 4:5 | The top and bottom of a vertical video |
| Facebook feed | 4:5 or 1:1 | Same |
| Pinterest | 2:3 vertical | The bottom third of a 9:16 |

Take a 9:16 video with your price typed across the bottom, post it to a feed that crops to 4:5, and the price is gone. Take the same video to Pinterest and the bottom third goes with it.

There is a second crop nobody warns you about: the platform's own interface. On a vertical feed the bottom of the frame is under the caption, the username and a row of buttons, and the right edge is under the like and share column. Text you put there is legible in your editor and invisible in the feed.

So keep every word that matters, the price, the product name, the shop link, inside the middle of the frame. Not the top, which gets covered by the status bar and the "following / for you" row. Not the bottom, which gets covered by everything else. The middle, in the safest ninety percent of the height and eighty percent of the width. Build it once that way and it survives every export.

## "Link in bio" is wrong on at least two of your platforms

This is the tell. Not the watermark, the caption.

"Link in bio" means something on TikTok and Instagram. On a YouTube description it is nonsense, because the link can just be there, in the text, tappable. On Pinterest the whole pin is a link. On Facebook you can put the URL directly into the post. Writing "link in bio" on those platforms tells a reader you copied this from somewhere else and did not look at it again.

The same applies to platform-specific words. "Comment SIZE and I'll send the link" reads fine on Instagram and reads odd on a Short. "Swipe up" has not been the mechanic on Stories for years. "Duet this" means nothing off TikTok.

Rewrite the first line of the caption for each platform and leave the rest. That is the whole job. The first line is what shows before the "more" cut anyway, so it is the only line most people read, and it is the one that has to be native.

> A stranger cannot tell whether your product is good. They can tell instantly whether you were talking to them or to somebody else, and a recycled caption answers that question before your video does.

## Timing, and the platform that runs on a different clock

Three of your four platforms are about today. One is not.

TikTok, Instagram and Facebook are broadly same-week machines. A post lands, does most of its numbers within a few days, and then mostly stops, though TikTok in particular will occasionally resurrect something from four months ago for reasons nobody has ever satisfactorily explained.

Pinterest is not that. A pin can surface for the first time weeks after you post it and keep bringing people for a year. Which means cross-posting a "back in stock today, three left" post to Pinterest is a small trap you set for yourself: in November somebody finds it, gets excited, and arrives at a sold-out page. Pins should be evergreen. The product, not the drop. The way that changes what you post there is covered in [selling on Pinterest without a shop](/en/blog/selling-on-pinterest-without-a-shop).

The practical rule: anything with a date, a countdown or a stock number in it does not get cross-posted to an evergreen surface. Everything else does.

## One asset, four exports, twenty minutes

Here is the routine that actually holds up over months, written in the order you do it.

1. **Film once, in 9:16**, with the important stuff in the middle of the frame.
2. **Edit in an app that is not a social platform.** Anything that exports a clean file.
3. **Export four times**: 9:16 for TikTok, Reels and Shorts; 4:5 for feed posts; 2:3 with a still frame and readable text for Pinterest; and keep the master.
4. **Write four first lines.** Same product, same price, different opening sentence, and no phrase that names a feature the platform does not have.
5. **Post the vertical version to TikTok first**, then Reels, then Shorts, spaced across the day rather than the minute. Not because anyone has proved the spacing matters, but because it stops you posting a broken export to four places at once.
6. **Same shop link everywhere.** Same string, same spelling, no per-platform variants.

That last point is the one that compounds. Different destinations on different platforms means you can never answer the question "where did that order come from", and it means a buyer who saw you on TikTok and searched for you later lands somewhere unfamiliar. The case for a single destination is in [one link for every platform](/en/blog/one-link-for-every-platform).

## Mika in Cebu, ₱850 crochet bags

Mika makes crochet market bags in Cebu, ₱850 each, ₱1,400 for a bag and a matching pouch. Her buyers are almost all Philippine, most of them pay cash on delivery, and Facebook is by a distance her biggest source of orders, which surprises people who assume everything happens on TikTok.

For eight months she posted the identical file to Facebook, Instagram and TikTok, saved out of whichever app she had edited in. The TikTok version had a watermark on the Reel. The Reel version was a 9:16 squeezed into a feed post, so the ₱850 she had typed at the bottom was cropped off on Facebook, which is where most of her buyers were. She was, in effect, hiding the price from her best audience while congratulating herself on being efficient.

What she changed took one Sunday:

1. She started editing in a separate app and exporting twice, 9:16 and 4:5, with the price in the middle of the frame in both.
2. Facebook captions got the URL typed out in full, because Facebook lets you. TikTok and Instagram captions got the shop name said as a word instead.
3. She stopped posting stock counts anywhere except Stories, which disappear anyway.
4. Every caption's first line became the same shape: what it is, the price, the city she ships from.

Her order count roughly doubled over two months on the same amount of filming. Almost all of the increase came from Facebook, and her own explanation is the boring one: people could finally see the price without asking.

She still gets the question. Just from twelve people a week instead of sixty.

## When cross-posting is the wrong instinct

Two cases, both common.

**You are on four platforms and none of them is working.** Cross-posting a weak idea to more places gets you more views of a weak idea. If nothing is selling on your best platform, adding three more is a way of feeling busy. Fix one first. [Which platform should you actually sell on](/en/blog/which-platform-should-you-actually-sell-on) is the less comfortable version of that conversation.

**The formats are genuinely different, not just differently shaped.** A Story is a conversation with people who already follow you: polls, questions, a face, a sticker, gone in a day. A TikTok is a pitch to strangers. Running the same fifteen seconds through both is not cross-posting, it is using the wrong tool in one of them. What Stories are actually for is covered in [selling through Instagram Stories](/en/blog/selling-through-instagram-stories), and it is not a place to put your Reels.

There is also a version of this that is purely about your week. Four platforms done properly is not four times the work of one, but it is not the same work either, and a seller doing eleven orders a month should be on one platform doing it well. The reach ceiling is much higher than most people's fulfilment ceiling.

## Do this with your next post

1. Open your last four posts and find the one with a watermark on it. That is the one to fix first.
2. Check whether your price is visible in the feed crop on the platform where most of your orders come from. On a phone, not in your editor.
3. Rewrite the first line of each caption so it names something the platform actually has.
4. Delete "link in bio" anywhere the platform lets you put an actual link.
5. Make the shop link identical everywhere and then never touch it again.

Do that for a month and you will know which platform is carrying you, which is the only thing worth knowing before you decide where next Sunday goes.
