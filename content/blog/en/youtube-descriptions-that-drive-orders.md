Your video has 1,800 views, three comments asking what it costs, and your shop link is sitting on line nine of the description underneath a paragraph about your camera gear.

Nobody scrolled that far. Almost nobody ever does.

The description is not a footer. It is the only tappable thing on the page you fully control, and roughly the first hundred characters of it are doing all the work. So put the shop link and the price in the opening line, above the point where YouTube cuts you off with "...more". Repeat it in a pinned comment. Say the shop name out loud in the video. Everything under the fold is for search and for people who were already going to buy. A Sailo shop is $0 on the free plan for up to 20 products and lives at `sailo.store/yourname`, which matters here because it is short enough to fit in that first line with a price beside it.

## The fold is about a hundred characters and you should go and measure yours

On a phone, YouTube shows a couple of lines of description under the title before it truncates. Call it a hundred characters. On desktop it is more generous, somewhere near a hundred and fifty. Both numbers have moved over the years and they will move again.

Do not trust that paragraph, including the version of it in this article. Open one of your own videos on your own phone, right now, and count the characters you can see without tapping anything. That is your real estate. It is smaller than you think and it is the only part of a 5,000-character field that most viewers will ever read.

What most sellers put there: the video title again, or a greeting, or "Welcome back to the channel."

What should be there: what you sell, what it costs, and where to get it.

## Write line one as a sentence, not a URL dump

There is a real difference between these two openers, and it is not cosmetic.

```
Shop link below!
https://sailo.store/mokabean
```

```
Order the 500g Nyeri roast, KSh 1,200, at sailo.store/mokabean
```

The first one is a chore. It tells the viewer that work is required and gives them nothing to decide with. The second one is a complete transaction described in eleven words, and a viewer who reads it has already made most of the decision before their thumb moves.

Two rules for that line:

1. **A number in it.** A price, a size, a delivery window. Something concrete. A description with no number in it is a slogan.
2. **The link as readable text, not a wall of URL.** `sailo.store/mokabean` reads as a name. A link with a tracking string on the end reads as an advert, and it eats forty of your hundred characters.

YouTube will make the URL tappable wherever it sits in the description, so you do not need to put it on its own line to activate it. Write it into the sentence.

> The first line of a YouTube description is a shop sign, not a table of contents. If it does not contain a price, you are using the best space you own to say nothing.

## The pinned comment is a second front door

You can pin one comment on your own video, and it sits at the top of the comment thread on every device.

Most sellers waste it. They pin "thanks for watching" or a link that duplicates the description word for word. Both are a shame, because the pinned comment does a job the description cannot: it answers the question that would otherwise arrive forty times.

If the video is about coffee, pin the grind options and the roast date. If it is about a dress, pin the sizes in stock today. If it is about a service, pin what you charge and how far you travel.

Then edit it. When something sells out, change the pin to say so. It takes six seconds and it stops a week of "is the sage one back yet". A pinned comment is the only part of a published video you can update without re-uploading anything, and sellers who treat it as live inventory get more out of one video than sellers who treat it as a signature.

One caution. Whether a link inside a comment renders as tappable has varied by channel and by the moderation settings on your own channel, and it can look fine to you while looking like plain text to a stranger. Check it in an incognito window or on a friend's phone before you build a strategy on it. If it comes out flat, the pin still earns its place as the answers slot; the tappable path is the description and the channel page.

## What goes below the fold, and in what order

Nobody reads down there for pleasure. Two audiences do read it: YouTube's own systems, working out what the video is about, and the small group of viewers who tapped "more" because they had already decided to buy.

Write for both, in this order:

1. **Two or three sentences describing the video in the words a buyer would search.** Not "unboxing my new gear". Something like "how I roast and pack single-origin Kenyan coffee for delivery in Nairobi". Plain, literal, keyword-shaped without being stuffed.
2. **The product list with prices.** One line each. This is the part the decided buyer came for.
3. **Delivery and timing.** When you ship, what it costs, where you cover.
4. **Chapters, if the video is long enough to need them.**
5. **Everything else.** Socials, gear, music credits, the channel disclaimer.

If your description currently starts at item five, you are not unusual. Most channels are built that way because most channels were built by people making videos rather than selling things.

## Shorts behave differently, and the difference costs sellers weeks

A Short has a description field, but the viewer experience is a vertical feed with a thumb already moving. What is visible before the "more" tap is a sliver, and the behaviour of links inside Shorts descriptions has changed more than once.

Rather than betting on it, do what sellers who make Shorts actually do:

- Put the shop name **on screen** as text for the last three seconds.
- **Say it out loud.** Voice survives a muted autoplay less well than text, but it survives a screenshot and a memory better than a link does.
- Keep the first line of the description short enough to read in the sliver, with the price in it.
- Send everything else to the channel page, where your links live in a place that does not change per-video.

Test whether the link in your own Shorts description is tappable, from a phone that is not signed in as you. Two minutes of that is worth more than any article's assertion about current behaviour, this one included.

## The link people never tap is the one that earns most

Here is the part that takes about thirty uploads to work out on your own.

A large share of the people who buy because of a YouTube video do not tap anything. They watch on a TV in the evening. They watch on a laptop with fourteen tabs open. They watch, they want it, and two days later they remember the product and not where they saw it.

If the only path back to you is a link they did not tap, that sale is gone. If your channel is `MokaBean` and your shop is `sailo.store/mokabean`, they can find you from memory, from a screenshot, from a friend saying "that coffee guy in Nairobi".

So make the string identical everywhere: channel handle, shop link, Instagram, the last three seconds of every video. Boring, and it beats every clever link placement, because a link only works while the viewer is still watching.

A related trap: editing the description of a video that already did well feels like it does nothing. YouTube does not re-notify anybody, so the traffic from the edit arrives as a slow drip over days rather than a spike in an hour, and most sellers conclude the change failed and put the old text back a week before it would have shown up. Give an edit a fortnight before you judge it.

## Cards and end screens are probably not available to you yet

In Studio you will see options for cards and end screens. They look like the obvious answer.

Pointing either of them at your own website is gated. External links from cards and end screens have long required Partner Program membership and an approved associated website, which for a seller with 400 subscribers means not yet. Open Studio and try to add one. If the field is greyed out or asks you to verify a website you do not have, you have your answer in thirty seconds and you can stop reading guides that assume you are a 100k channel.

Your channel page links are the ungated version. They show on your profile, they survive every video, and they are where a viewer who searched your name lands.

## Wanjiru in Nairobi, 500g bags at KSh 1,200

Wanjiru roasts coffee in Nairobi and sells it in 500g bags at KSh 1,200, plus a KSh 650 sample trio. Her channel is small, about 2,900 subscribers, and she uploads twice a month: roasting, grinding, one video a year about water temperature that does better than anything else she makes.

For her first eighteen months her descriptions opened with "Hi everyone, welcome back". Her shop link was in the middle of a block of text under three hashtags. She was getting maybe six orders a month from a channel that regularly did four-figure view counts, and she assumed YouTube viewers just did not buy things.

What she changed, in this order:

1. First line of every description became `Order the 500g Nyeri roast, KSh 1,200, at sailo.store/mokabean` and nothing else went above the fold.
2. Pinned comment became the grind options plus the roast date for that week, updated every Monday.
3. Last four seconds of each video became her shop name on screen while she said it.
4. Channel handle changed to match the shop link exactly.

Orders went to roughly twenty a month on the same upload schedule. The interesting part was where they came from. Her link clicks went up modestly. The bigger change was people arriving at the shop having typed the name in, days after watching, sometimes from a video that was two years old. That traffic did not exist before, because before there was nothing to type.

She also stopped getting the grind question. It was in the pin.

## What this costs, and where Sailo will not help you

Sailo's free plan is $0, holds 20 products, and takes no commission at all on bank transfer, cash on delivery, WhatsApp, Instagram, Telegram, email or phone orders. Sailo never touches the money on those rails, so there is nothing to take a cut of.

Card payments are the paid part. They need the Business plan at $19.99 a month plus a Stripe account that Stripe has cleared for charges, and Sailo takes 0.5% of the goods on each card order, after any discount and excluding delivery and tax. On Wanjiru's KSh 1,200 bag that is about six shillings to Sailo, on top of Stripe's own fee. The six shillings is not the problem. The $19.99 is, if you are doing twenty orders a month.

And for Wanjiru specifically, the honest answer is that Sailo has no M-Pesa rail. None. Her buyers expect to pay a till number and that is not a payment method Sailo offers. What she does instead is put the till number into the bank transfer instructions field, where the buyer sees it at checkout and pays exactly as they always have. Sailo handles the catalogue, the options and the order record. She matches the payments to the orders herself, in the evening, off her own statement. Sailo cannot tell her the money arrived. Only Safaricom can.

That is a real limitation and it is worth knowing before you spend a Saturday setting anything up.

## Do this to your next upload

In this order, before you hit publish:

1. Open your last video on your phone and count the characters visible before "...more".
2. Rewrite the first line as one sentence containing what it is, the price, and the shop name. Delete the greeting.
3. Pin a comment with the answer to the question you get most, and put a date in it.
4. Put the shop name on screen for the last three seconds and say it out loud.
5. Go back and fix the first line on your three best-performing old videos. Then leave them alone for two weeks.

That last step is the one people skip, and it is the one with the most view-hours behind it.
